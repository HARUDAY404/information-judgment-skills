# Content Lab

Transform a judgment into a public expression and a learnable experiment. Optimize for the intended audience outcome while preserving evidence boundaries.

## Choose the mode

- `validated`: the central claim has adequate evidence for the wording used;
- `exploratory`: the creator is openly testing a hypothesis or inviting counterexamples.

Never present exploratory material as settled. A judgment can be valuable content without being certain when uncertainty is part of the design.

## Define the audience job

Specify the person, situation, recurring problem, current belief, and desired change. Avoid demographic labels without a concrete use context. State the one primary cognitive or behavioral change intended after consumption.

## Build the content contract

Use [references/experiment-schema.md](references/experiment-schema.md) to define:

- the originating `judgment_id` and a stable `experiment_id`;
- one central claim;
- evidence and visible proof;
- uncertainty and applicability boundary;
- why the creator has standing to discuss it;
- the most suitable structure and format;
- desired action or response;
- one primary experiment variable;
- one primary metric plus relevant guardrail metrics;
- success, failure, and learning criteria.

Select an explanatory path that fits the material, such as misconception correction, public experiment, claim audit, system build log, or personal belief revision. Do not impose a viral template on every topic.

## Draft honestly

The headline and opening may create curiosity but must be fully paid off. Preserve distinctions among fact, inference, experience, and promotion. Use concrete examples and original evidence where available. Do not erase the creator's uncertainty, disagreement, or natural language merely to sound authoritative.

Before finalizing, verify that the content does not strengthen the source judgment, generalize beyond its population, hide costs or failures, or imply causality without support.

## Learn after publication

Evaluate the result against the intended layer:

- attention: click, hold, completion;
- comprehension: accurate paraphrase and recurring misunderstanding;
- trust: saves, profile exploration, citations, substantive messages;
- relationship: repeat participation and qualified follows;
- action: use, replication, signup, inquiry, or purchase when relevant.

Engagement demonstrates audience behavior under specific conditions, not truth. Extract new questions, counterexamples, and evidence. Send belief-relevant feedback back to `judgment-journal`; send disputed factual claims to `claim-auditor`.

## Boundaries

- Do not change the conclusion to improve reach.
- Do not infer a general rule from one successful post.
- Do not optimize on likes alone.
- Do not change many major variables and then claim to know which caused the result.
- Do not publish, schedule, message, or otherwise act externally without explicit authorization.
