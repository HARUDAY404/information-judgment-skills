# Evidence Rubric

Keep dimensions separate. Use 0–3 with a concise reason when scoring helps comparison; `unknown` is valid.

- `source_proximity`: distance from the original event, data, or record;
- `method_quality`: suitability and transparency of sampling, measurement, comparison, and analysis;
- `directness`: whether the evidence tests the claim as worded;
- `independence`: whether supporting sources have distinct origins and incentives;
- `replicability`: whether methods, conditions, or results can be checked or repeated;
- `counterevidence_resilience`: how the claim fares against credible alternatives and failures;
- `scope_fit`: match between the studied population or conditions and the claimed generalization.

Also record, without blending into one number:

- material conflicts or promotional incentives;
- unavailable evidence;
- uncertainty caused by recency;
- whether the claim is descriptive, causal, predictive, or normative.

## Evidence roles

- Direct evidence can support the stated relationship under defined conditions.
- Indirect evidence supports a premise or nearby relationship.
- A case establishes that something occurred, not its prevalence or cause.
- Testimony establishes what a person reports, not necessarily what occurred.
- Absence of accessible evidence is not automatically evidence of absence.

## Confidence language

Prefer calibrated verbal conclusions over an unexplained total score:

- strong direct support;
- moderate support with named limitations;
- weak or indirect support;
- mixed evidence;
- insufficient evidence;
- contradicted under specified conditions;
- not truth-evaluable without resolving values or definitions.
